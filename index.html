Built like a tool, not a toy

Everything a download manager should do — and nothing it shouldn't.

A multi-connection engine

Files are split into parallel streams and written straight into place. When the bar fills, the file is already whole — there is no merging step at the end.

Resume that survives restarts

Pause it, close the app, lose power. The journal is saved as you go, so your list comes back and every download resumes from the exact byte it reached.

Video, with a real picker

A pill on the video player opens a panel with the thumbnail, the title and every quality next to its true file size. Pick one, press Download. Nothing starts on its own.

A browser extension

For Chromium browsers and Firefox. It hands downloads and video pages to the app through a native bridge — and shows a live progress card on the page itself.

Speed Limiter, per file or global

Cap the whole app, or just one download, so your browsing stays smooth. The limit can be changed from the browser card while the file is still coming down.

Queues & scheduler

Group downloads into queues, start them at a set time, and cap how many run at once. Finished queues can even shut the machine down for you.

Categories & site logins

Downloads sort themselves into categories you can extend. Logins for sites you use are kept on your machine, so gated files download without re-typing passwords.

Glass light, proper dark

A light glass finish by default and a dark theme one click away. Same layout, same density of information — just the light changes.

From the browser, straight into NDM

The extension and the app work as one: find a video, pick a quality, and follow the download without ever leaving the page.

  1. Click the pillIt sits on the video player — one click opens the picker.
  2. Pick a qualityThumbnail, duration and the real size of every format, side by side.
  3. Press DownloadThe progress card takes over — pause, resume, or limit the speed right there.

The main window, the video picker and the progress card now share one light glass finish, and the download list carries status dots and an in-row progress bar. Questions, answered plainly

Is NDM free?

Yes — free to download and use, with no ads, no account and no trial period counting down.

Which platforms does it run on?

Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit), as an installer or a portable build. On Linux, any Debian-family 64-bit distribution — Ubuntu, Zorin OS, Linux Mint and relatives — as a .deb package.

How do updates work?

NDM checks the release feed when it starts and again every few hours while it runs. When a new version is out, the app offers it in one click and verifies the download's hash before installing. Some releases are marked as a minimum supported version; if yours falls below it, the app asks you to update before starting new downloads, so critical fixes reach everyone.

Can it resume a broken download?

Yes. Where the server supports resuming (most do), NDM continues from the exact byte — after a pause, a crash, or a full restart. If a link has expired, you can refresh it and carry on instead of starting over.

Which browsers does the extension support?

Chrome, Brave, Edge and other Chromium-based browsers, plus Firefox. The extension talks to the app through a small native bridge installed with NDM; the app needs to be installed for the extension to do its work.

Is my data sent anywhere?

Your downloads, history and saved site logins stay on your machine. The only calls NDM itself makes are the update check and a single anonymous install count the first time it runs — a counter going up by one, carrying no personal data, no file names and no URLs. There is no account and no analytics.
